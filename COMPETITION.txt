SHORTEST-PATH SPEED COMPETITION
(Chinese version: COMPETITION.zh-TW.txt   Full spec: README.md)

THE TASK
  You are given a working Dijkstra, dijkstra_foundation.cpp. Copy it to
  solver.cpp and make it faster. Same output, less time. How many times
  faster you are IS your score.

KEY FACTS
  Hand in    solver.cpp and result.json (both from the same run)
  Where      https://shortestpaths.ccu2026algorithm.workers.dev
  Deadline   27 Sep 2026, 23:59 Taipei. Late uploads are refused.
  Score      geometric mean of your six speedups over the baseline
  Tries      unlimited; your best score ranks
  Limits     C++17, standard library only, single file, single thread,
             4 GB, 3600 s per instance

FOUR COMMANDS
  make foundation                        # build the baseline
  scripts/download_large.sh              # scored data, 185 MB, once
  cp dijkstra_foundation.cpp solver.cpp  # this file is your assignment
  make solver                            # ...edit, rebuild...
  python3 grade.py --solver ./solver --instances instances.txt \
      --json result.json                 # score

  The "Overall geomean" line is your score. Check your toolchain first
  with "make check-data". The scored run costs the baseline about 1.7 h
  of CPU, so develop against instances_dev.txt (~1.5 min); only
  instances.txt is scored.

THE SIX SCORED DATASETS
  Each is a different kind of graph: a trick that wins on one may do
  nothing on the next. Two are LOCAL (short-range queries), four are
  GLOBAL (long-range). Per-instance details are in instances/*.meta.json.

  road2d_large     GLOBAL   300,304 V  1,629,732 E  200,000 Q
      Directed road network with coordinates, 8% one-way. Targets at
      Dijkstra ranks 2^8-2^17.
  lattice3d_large  GLOBAL   300,763 V    902,289 E   30,000 Q
      3-D torus lattice, degree 6, no coordinates, weights 1-10^6.
      Uniform random pairs.
  local2d_large    LOCAL    300,304 V    600,608 E  300,000 Q
      2-D torus lattice, weights 1-1000. 90% of queries are short-range
      (target within rank 4096), 10% uniform.
  scalefree_large  GLOBAL   300,000 V    873,003 E   50,000 Q
      Directed power-law graph, 3% sinks, weights 1-10^4. 70% uniform,
      30% into the top 0.1% hubs; ~3% of targets unreachable (print -1).
  hugeq_large      GLOBAL    50,176 V    271,492 E  600,000 Q
      The small road graph, but 600,000 uniform queries.
  wide64_large     LOCAL   1,999,396 V  3,998,792 E  30,000 Q
      2-D torus lattice, weights 10^8-10^9. Distances exceed 2^32, so
      32-bit distances overflow silently. Largest graph in the set.

  About 0.1% of queries have s == t (answer 0). The final grading uses
  fresh instances from the same generator with a different seed, so
  depending on the exact bytes of the released files will not carry over.

HOW THE SCORE WORKS
  Per instance:  speedup = T_base / T_solver, both timed by grade.py on
  your own machine in the same run, so machine speed cancels out.
  Score = (s1 * s2 * s3 * s4 * s5 * s6) ^ (1/6).

  No cap: 400x faster scores 400.
  A failed instance scores 0.1 and is never dropped. In the README's
  example, five good instances are worth ~49x but one WRONG pulls the
  score down to 17.4x. Correctness first, then speed.
  Failure notes: WRONG, TIMEOUT (3600 s), MEMORY (4 GB), THREADS,
  NONDETERMINISTIC, CRASH, NOOUTPUT -- all 0.1.
  T_base is extrapolated from short probes (same constant for everyone,
  cancels in the ranking); your solver is run 3 times, best time kept.

THE RULES
  1. solver.cpp must be a modified copy of dijkstra_foundation.cpp,
     not a rewrite.
  2. Keep the command line: ./solver <graph> <queries> <output>.
  3. Output exactly what the baseline outputs (-1 if unreachable).
  4. C++17, standard library only.
  5. One file. No headers of your own.
  6. Single-threaded.
  7. Under 4 GB and 3600 s per instance.
  8. No precomputed answers.
  Do not edit dijkstra_foundation.cpp, grade.py, or instances/.
  grade.py enforces 3, 6 and 7; 1, 4, 5 and 8 are checked by reading
  your solver.cpp -- which is why the source is uploaded with the score.

THE SUBMISSION SITE
  https://shortestpaths.ccu2026algorithm.workers.dev
  A public scoreboard on Cloudflare: no account, no VPN, works anywhere.

  The board shows a live countdown, the pinned baseline row at 1.00x,
  and one row per student: overall score, Global and Local means, all
  six per-instance speedups, tries, and last upload time. Overall /
  Global / Local switch the ranking key; Overall is the official one.
  Student IDs are public; your source code is not.

  To upload: click Submit (top right), enter your Student ID exactly as
  in the course roster, choose result.json and solver.cpp, then click
  "Submit and view ranking".

  The server re-derives your score from the times in result.json, so
  files not produced by "grade.py --json", or whose numbers do not add
  up, are rejected. result.json stores the SHA-256 of the solver.cpp it
  measured, so the two files must come from the same run. Your
  solver.cpp is kept for the instructor only.

  Submit early and often -- a bad attempt costs nothing, and only your
  best score ranks. Do not leave it to the last hour: a full scored run
  takes a long time.

BEFORE YOU UPLOAD
  make check-data says ok; git diff dijkstra_foundation.cpp is empty;
  all six rows of the scored run say ok; and you upload that
  result.json together with that solver.cpp.

Questions: ask before the deadline rather than guessing. Where this
page and README.md disagree, README.md wins.
